## Learning Focus

Read two-way frequency tables and use joint, marginal, and conditional relative frequencies to look for association between categorical variables.

## Big idea

A **two-way table** counts individuals by two categories.

- **Joint** frequency: both conditions together.
- **Marginal** frequency: row or column totals.
- **Conditional** relative frequency: a fraction within one row or column.
- If conditional percentages differ across groups, there is an **association**.

## Worked example

Survey of $100$ students on liking pizza:

- Grade 9: $30$ like, $10$ do not (total $40$).
- Grade 10: $20$ like, $40$ do not (total $60$).

P(likes | grade 9) $=\frac{30}{40}=0.75$; P(likes | grade 10) $=\frac{20}{60}\approx0.33$. The difference shows an association.

## Takeaways

- Use the right denominator for conditional frequencies.
- Different conditional rates suggest association.
- Describe the result in context.
