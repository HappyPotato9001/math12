## Learning Focus

Model situations where the **rate of change changes** using piecewise descriptions and graphs.

## Big idea

When the rate changes, the graph is made of different pieces:

- Each piece is linear with its own slope.
- The pieces connect at the input where the rate changes.
- The **later piece** continues from the previous output:
  $$f(t)=f(t_0)+m(t-t_0)\quad\text{for }t\ge t_0$$

A smaller slope means the graph is still rising, but less steeply.

## Worked example

A tank fills $4$ gal/min for the first $10$ min, then $2$ gal/min.

- $0\le t\le10$: $V(t)=4t$, so $V(10)=40$
- $t\ge10$: $V(t)=40+2(t-10)$
- $V(15)=40+10=50$
- Reaches $80$: $40+2(t-10)=80\Rightarrow t=30$

## Takeaways

- Find the value where the rate changes, then continue from it.
- Use $f(t_0)+m(t-t_0)$ for each piece.
- A slope decreasing in size means the graph flattens, not falls.
