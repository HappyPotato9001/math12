---
title: Symmetries of Quadrilaterals
---

## Learning Focus

Classify quadrilaterals by their lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry.

## Big idea

A figure has **line symmetry** if a reflection maps it onto itself and **rotational symmetry** if a rotation of less than $360^\circ$ does.

- Square: $4$ lines, rotations by $90^\circ$.
- Rectangle and rhombus (non-square): $2$ lines, rotation by $180^\circ$.
- Isosceles trapezoid: $1$ line.
- General parallelogram: $0$ lines, but $180^\circ$ rotational symmetry.

## Worked example

A square has $4$ lines of symmetry (two diagonals, two midlines). Its smallest rotation onto itself is $\frac{360^\circ}{4}=90^\circ$.

A parallelogram that is not a rectangle or rhombus has no reflection symmetry, but a $180^\circ$ turn about its center maps it to itself.

## Takeaways

- Count lines of symmetry by trying reflections.
- Rotational symmetry angle is $360^\circ/n$.
- Special quadrilaterals have more symmetry.
