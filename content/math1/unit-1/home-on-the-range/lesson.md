title: Home on the Range
---

## Learning Focus

Describe the **domain** and **range** of a sequence and see why they are different from those of a continuous function.

## Big idea

A sequence $f(n)$ is a function whose **domain** is the term numbers: $n=1,2,3,\dots$ (positive integers, or a finite list if the sequence stops).

The **range** is the set of output values (the terms themselves).

- The graph is a set of separate **points**, not a connected line.
- The domain is *discrete*: there is no term $2.5$.
- The range is also a list of separate values.

## Worked example

Let $f(n)=2n+1$ for $n=1,2,3,\dots$

- Domain: $\{1,2,3,\dots\}$
- Range: $\{3,5,7,9,\dots\}$ (odd numbers starting at 3)

If we only look at the first 5 terms, the domain is $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ and the range is $\{3,5,7,9,11\}$.

A line $y=2x+1$ would allow *every* real $x$; the sequence does not.

## Takeaways

- Domain of a sequence = term numbers (positive integers).
- Range = the actual terms produced.
- Sequence graphs are dots; the domain restriction is what makes them different from lines.
