title: I Know; What Do You Know?
---

## Learning Focus

Identify whether a sequence is **arithmetic**, **geometric**, or **neither**, and write a rule when possible.

## Big idea

Test a sequence in two steps:

1. Compute differences $f(n+1)-f(n)$. All equal? $\Rightarrow$ **arithmetic**.
2. Compute ratios $\dfrac{f(n+1)}{f(n)}$. All equal? $\Rightarrow$ **geometric**.

If neither is constant, the sequence is **neither** (for example, square numbers).

## Worked example

Classify $1,4,9,16,\dots$

- Differences: $3,5,7$ — not constant
- Ratios: $4,\ 2.25,\ 1.78\dots$ — not constant
- So the sequence is **neither**.

Classify $20,17,14,11$: differences are all $-3$, so it is arithmetic with $f(n)=20-3(n-1)=23-3n$.

## Takeaways

- Check differences first, then ratios.
- Check **all** consecutive pairs, not just the first two.
- Some sequences are neither; that's okay.
