---
title: What Comes Next? What Comes Later?
---

## Learning Focus

Compare **arithmetic** growth (adding) with **geometric** growth (multiplying) for near and far terms.

## Big idea

- **Arithmetic**: add a constant difference $d$. Explicit: $f(n)=f(1)+d(n-1)$. Graph points lie on a line.
- **Geometric**: multiply by a constant ratio $r$. Explicit: $f(n)=f(1)\cdot r^{\,n-1}$. Graph points curve upward (for $r>1$).

Early terms can look similar, but with $r>1$ a geometric sequence **eventually** outgrows any arithmetic sequence.

## Worked example

Compare $5,8,11,\dots$ (add 3) with $5,10,20,\dots$ (double).

| $n$ | arithmetic | geometric |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | 5 |
| 2 | 8 | 10 |
| 3 | 11 | 20 |
| 10 | $5+3(9)=32$ | $5\cdot2^9=2560$ |

Same start, very different "later."

## Takeaways

- Constant differences mean arithmetic; constant ratios mean geometric.
- Use explicit formulas to answer "what comes later?"
- Growth by a factor greater than 1 eventually beats growth by adding.
