---
title: What Does It Mean?
---

## Learning Focus

Find missing terms in an arithmetic sequence (**arithmetic means**) when the endpoints are known.

## Big idea

If you know two terms of an arithmetic sequence with $k$ missing terms between them, there are $k+1$ equal steps from the first to the last.

$$d=\frac{\text{last}-\text{first}}{k+1}$$

Then add $d$ repeatedly to fill in the gaps. The inserted terms are called **arithmetic means**.

## Worked example

Insert 3 arithmetic means between $5$ and $29$.

- There are $3+1=4$ steps.
- $d=\dfrac{29-5}{4}=6$
- Sequence: $5,\ 11,\ 17,\ 23,\ 29$

Check: each step adds 6 and the last term is 29 ✓.

## Takeaways

- With $k$ missing terms there are $k+1$ steps.
- Divide the total change by the number of steps to get $d$.
- Always check that the endpoint matches.
