title: Be the Change
---

## Learning Focus

Find and interpret average rates of change for quadratic functions.

## Big idea

Average rate of change of $f$ from $x=a$ to $x=b$:

$$\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}.$$

For a linear function this is constant. For a quadratic it **depends on the interval**. For $f(x)=x^2$ from $a$ to $a+h$:

$$\frac{(a+h)^2-a^2}{h}=\frac{2ah+h^2}{h}=2a+h.$$

## Worked example

$f(x)=x^2$ from $x=1$ to $x=4$:

$$\frac{16-1}{3}=5.$$

(Check with $2a+h=2(1)+3=5$.)

## Takeaways

- Average rate of change is the slope of the secant line.
- Quadratics have non-constant rates of change.
- A rate of $0$ over an interval means the function returns to the same value (net change zero).
