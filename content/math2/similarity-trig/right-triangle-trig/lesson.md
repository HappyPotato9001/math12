---
title: Right-Triangle Trig
---

## The idea

For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle:

- $\sin \theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
- $\cos \theta = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
- $\tan \theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$

Remember **SOH-CAH-TOA**.

## Worked example

In a right triangle, the side opposite $\theta$ is 5 and the hypotenuse is 13.

$\sin \theta = \dfrac{5}{13}$.

If you need the adjacent side, use Pythagoras: $adj = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = 12$, so $\cos \theta = \dfrac{12}{13}$.

## Common mistakes

- Swapping opposite and adjacent for the chosen angle.
- Using a non-hypotenuse side in sine/cosine by accident.
- Mixing degree and radian mode on a calculator (keep degrees in Math 2 unless told otherwise).
